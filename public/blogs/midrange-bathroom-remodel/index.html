How Much Does a Midrange Bathroom Remodel Cost?

By RT Signature Homes Design & Build Editorial Team  ·  3 min read

A midrange bathroom remodel typically costs between $15,000 and $35,000 nationwide, with most homeowners landing near $20,000 to $28,000 for a standard midrange scope. Midrange means mid-quality fixtures and finishes, new tile and shower or tub, updated plumbing and electrical to code, and cosmetic improvements without major structural reconfiguration.

If that range fits your budget band, keep reading for a clear scope, two practical example budgets, the main cost drivers to watch, a realistic schedule, and a simple decision guide for whether to hire a design-build firm or manage multiple trades yourself.

TL;DR

  • A U.S.-wide midrange bathroom remodel usually ranges from $15,000 to $35,000 depending on size and choices.
  • Biggest budget movers are layout changes, plumbing/electrical moves, and labor-intensive tile work.
  • A phased timeline of 3 to 6 weeks is typical; include a contingency of 10 to 15 percent for unseen conditions.
  • Hiring a single design-build firm increases cost predictability and reduces coordination risk compared with DIY or multiple contractors.
  • Prepare measurements, a priorities list, and a budget band before requesting written estimates.

Primary cost factors that change a midrange budget

These are the levers that will move a midrange estimate up or down. Read each item to understand where to spend or save.

Layout and structural changes

Moving walls, changing the location of the toilet or tub, or opening a wall adds framing, drywall, and possible structural permitting fees. Minor layout tweaks are common in midrange projects and can add several thousand dollars.

Plumbing and electrical

Relocating drains or adding new circuits raises costs more than fixtures themselves. Expect higher charges if plumbers must chase pipes in existing walls or if old wiring needs replacement to meet code.

Material quality

Midrange typically uses mid-tier vanities, cultured stone or quartz counters, porcelain tile, and reliable fixtures. Premium natural stone or custom cabinetry pushes you into high-end pricing.

Labor intensity

Tile work, waterproofing, and complex shower installations are labor-heavy. In many markets labor will be 40 to 60 percent of your total cost for a full remodel.

Waterproofing and substrate prep

Proper waterproofing under tile and around wet areas is non-negotiable. Skipping it can save money short term and cost far more later.

Permits and inspections

Permits vary by jurisdiction but are required for most full bathroom remodels. Budget for permit fees and the time associated with inspections.

Unseen repairs

Older homes often reveal rot, mold, or framing issues during demo. A midrange estimate should include a 10 to 15 percent contingency for these surprises.

Expert note: protect long-term value

Expert note: Invest in proper waterproofing, a good exhaust fan, and durable grout and tile installation. These items cost a little more upfront and materially lower long-term maintenance and repair costs. Avoid chasing trendy, expensive finishes for elements that are better replaced later, like faucets and mirrors. Prioritize systems and substrates, then aesthetics.

Common warning signs

Watch for this: If a bid lacks line-item detail, asks for the full balance upfront, or refuses to show permit paperwork, treat it as high risk. Insist on a written contract with phased payments tied to milestones, and keep a 5 to 10 percent holdback until final punch list completion.

Typical midrange remodel timeline and payment milestones

A realistic schedule helps you plan living arrangements and cash flow. Below are standard milestones and when costs typically occur.

  1. 1Design and estimate
    1 to 2 weeks for measurements, design direction, and a written estimate. You decide fixtures and finishes during this phase.
  2. 2Permits and procurement
    1 to 3 weeks depending on local permit turnaround and lead times for ordered items like vanities and tile.
  3. 3Demolition
    1 to 3 days. Payment milestone: small deposit for demo or first draw after initial payment depending on contract.
  4. 4Rough-in plumbing and electrical
    3 to 7 days. Inspections may be required before proceeding.
  5. 5Installations (tile, fixtures, cabinetry)
    1 to 2 weeks for tile and shower, plus time for counters and cabinetry installation.
  6. 6Finishes and punch list
    2 to 5 days for paint, hardware, final trim, and correcting items identified on the punch list. Final payment typically due on sign-off.

Hire model comparison: design-build, general contractor, or DIY

Choose the approach that matches your appetite for risk, time, and coordination.

Hire modelPros / Cons
Design-build (single firm handling design and trades)Pros: Single point of responsibility, better cost predictability, coordinated schedule. Cons: Slightly higher upfront design fee but fewer change orders and surprises.
General contractor coordinating separate designers and tradesPros: Flexible hiring, potential to shop trades. Cons: You manage more communication, higher chance of scheduling conflicts and hidden costs.
DIY or managing subcontractors yourselfPros: Lower direct labor costs if you have skills. Cons: High risk of costly mistakes, longer timeline, permit and inspection complexity.

What to have ready before you request quotes

Bring these items to get accurate, comparable estimates quickly.

  • ✓Exact room measurements (length, width, ceiling height) and a photo of each wall
  • ✓A must-have list: shower or tub, double vanity, heated floor, accessibility needs
  • ✓A budget band in mind (for example, $15k to $25k) and which items you will upgrade if budget allows
  • ✓Note any access constraints such as parking, staircase, or elevator issues for materials delivery
  • ✓List of non-negotiables (durable tile, secondary bathroom availability during work, ventilation upgrade)

Frequently asked questions

How much to have a bathroom remodel?

A midrange bathroom remodel typically costs between $15,000 and $35,000 in the U.S., depending on room size, layout changes, and materials. Small cosmetic remodels can be lower, while projects that move plumbing or require structural work are higher. Ask for a written phased estimate that separates materials, labor, permits, and a contingency.

How much does B&Q charge to fit a bathroom?

B&Q pricing varies by service package and location. Retail contractors often offer fixed-price packages for fitted bathrooms; compare what is included carefully (materials, labor, and permits). For a local, design-focused estimate tailored to your layout and finish levels, request a site assessment from a regional design-build firm who will show a detailed scope and phased pricing.

Can you redo a bathroom for $5000?

You can refresh a bathroom for around $5,000 if you limit work to cosmetic updates: paint, replace vanity with an off-the-shelf unit, swap hardware, and refinish the tub rather than replace tile. Full midrange remodels with new tile, plumbing, and electrical will typically exceed $5,000.

Can I renovate a bathroom for $10,000?

A $10,000 budget can cover a modest remodel if you keep layout and plumbing locations the same and choose economical materials and fixtures. Expect trades and labor to be the largest portion of cost; get multiple written quotes and a clear scope to see what is achievable in that budget band.
